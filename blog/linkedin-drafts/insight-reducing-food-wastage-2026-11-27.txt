Case study: reducing-food-wastage
Published: 27 November 2026

---

Wei Keong believed he ran a tight kitchen. Nine years in the business. He watched his staff. He ordered carefully.

He was throwing away RM4,356 every single month.

Most F&B owners think food waste is a kitchen discipline problem — staff not following recipes, over-prepping, or careless portioning. But when you actually look at the data, the pattern is almost never random.

Pau Analytics reviewed six months of purchase records and four weeks of kitchen waste logs at Wei Keong's Chinese restaurant in Petaling Jaya. The finding was precise:

22% of his total food cost was going in the bin.

Just 4 ingredients accounted for 77% of that waste — whole fish (31%), bean sprouts (18%), roast pork (16%), and leafy vegetables (12%). Everything else was fine.

And it was not spread evenly across the week. Tuesday and Wednesday generated 2.1 times more waste than Saturday — the busiest day. The cause was structural: Monday orders were sized for projected weekly demand, but the first two days consistently underperformed those projections for the full six months studied.

After three adjustments — a split fish order, a renegotiated bean sprout schedule, and a simple daily prep guide — monthly food spend dropped from RM19,800 to RM17,400. The RM2,400 saving came from no new equipment, no extra staff, and no major process change.

Check your own kitchen. Which 3 or 4 items are generating most of your waste? The answer is probably already in your purchase records.
