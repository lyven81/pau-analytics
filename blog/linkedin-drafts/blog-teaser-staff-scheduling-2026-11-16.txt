Ahmad's restaurants were spending 37% of revenue on wages. The industry norm is 26–28%.

He thought the fix was obvious: cut staff or push more revenue. Eight months of scheduling data said something different.

The problem wasn't how many staff he had. It was when they were showing up.

Weekday afternoons from 2–5pm: he had 4–5 staff on the floor, serving 8–9 covers per hour. Friday and Saturday evenings from 6–10pm: he had 5 staff, serving 41 covers per hour.

The first window was costing RM4,200 a month in unnecessary wages.
The second was costing him revenue, repeat bookings, and 3-star reviews about slow service.

He had been reading the wrong number. Labour cost as a monthly total looked manageable. Labour cost by shift told a completely different story.

The two errors cancelled each other out in every payroll summary — which is exactly why the problem had gone undetected for a year.

The fix wasn't headcount. It was alignment.

If your labour costs feel high but you can't see where, the data already has the answer. You just haven't looked at it this way yet.
