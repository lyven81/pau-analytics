Blog: Why Your Best-Selling Products May Not Be Your Most Profitable
Published: 10 August 2026

---

Your accessories category has 60% margins. Your main product line runs at 30%.

Which one is making you more money?

Most retail owners would say accessories. The numbers look better. Every sale is more efficient on paper.

We analysed 60,000 transactions at a bicycle retail chain. The accessories category had margins consistently above 60%. It also contributed a fraction of the total profit.

The bikes, with lower percentage margins, were doing almost all of the real work. Not because they sold in higher volume — but because their price points were high enough for the margin to matter. A 30% margin on a RM3,000 item delivers far more money than a 60% margin on a RM15 water bottle.

The team had been allocating shelf space, promotions, and buying effort toward the wrong products. They were optimising for margin percentage when they should have been managing absolute profit contribution.

There was one more finding worth knowing: of all the levers tested — volume, delivery speed, stock levels — price was the single variable most strongly linked to profit. Not operationally complex changes. Just pricing discipline on the right products.

A targeted 3% increase on selected models delivered more profit improvement in one month than six months of inventory work had produced.

If you haven't looked at your product range this way, it might be worth it.
