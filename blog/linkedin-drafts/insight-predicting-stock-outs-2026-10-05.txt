THURSDAY — Pau Analytics Standalone Insight
Case Study: predicting-stock-outs
Published: 5 October 2026

---

Most stock-out problems are not random. They have a schedule.

We analysed 18 months of inventory records from a three-outlet minimarket chain in the Klang Valley — 450-plus SKUs, daily stock movement data. The goal was simple: find out why certain shelves kept going empty despite the owner having what looked like enough stock.

What we found: 73% of all stock-out incidents across the chain were concentrated in just 12 SKUs. And those 12 SKUs were running out in the same three-day window every single month — the three days immediately before payday.

Demand on those days was 2.8 times the weekly average. The reorder system had been calibrated for steady weekly demand. Nobody had ever adjusted it for the month-end surge.

The owner had been treating this as a quantity problem. He had raised his reorder threshold once already. It did not help, because more total stock does not fix the wrong timing.

The fix was a schedule change — weekly restocking on those 12 items instead of fortnightly, plus a month-end buffer at the highest-demand outlet. Stock-outs on those 12 items fell 64% in three months. No increase in total inventory spend.

If you have stock-outs that keep recurring on the same products, pull the incident dates. If they cluster around the same days of the week or month, you do not have a stock problem. You have a timing problem.

The pattern is usually findable in data you already have.
