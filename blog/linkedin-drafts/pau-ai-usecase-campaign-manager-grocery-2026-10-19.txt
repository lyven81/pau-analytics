Ah Keong runs a minimart in Subang Jaya. Three outlets, a regular customer base, and a WhatsApp broadcast list he has been sending every month for two years.

The message is always the same: "This week's promotion — buy RM50 and above, get a free pack of biscuits."

He sends it to 600 numbers. About 40 people respond. He has no idea if those 40 would have come in anyway.

Last month, Ah Keong's Campaign Manager did something different.

It split his 600 customers into three groups based on purchase history:

• Regular weekday shoppers (180 customers) — they buy essentials 3–4 times a week. They do not need a discount. They need a reminder on Tuesday morning: "Fresh stock arrived — Milo, Mamee, and your usual." Response rate from this group: 34%.

• Monthly bulk buyers (95 customers) — they come once a month and spend RM120+. The system sent them early access to a bulk-buy bundle before the weekend. 22 of 95 took it up. Average basket on those visits: RM148.

• Lapsed customers (130 customers who had not visited in 6 weeks) — they received a simple "we miss you" message with a RM5 voucher. 19 came back within the week.

Three messages. Three audiences. One afternoon of setup.

Ah Keong now runs targeted campaigns instead of broadcasts. His response rate went from 7% to 24%. His monthly WhatsApp promotion cost stayed the same.
