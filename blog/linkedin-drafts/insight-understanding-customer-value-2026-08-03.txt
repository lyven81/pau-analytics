63% of customers at this retailer bought once and never came back.

A bicycle shop came to us with 18,400 customers and more than 60,000 sales transactions. On paper, that looks like a healthy business. Revenue was coming in. The shop was busy.

But when we looked at where the profit actually came from, the picture changed completely.

Fewer than 2% of customers had bought four or more times. That small group — the repeat buyers — generated approximately $10.6 million in total profit.

The medium-value group contributed $1.4 million.

The majority of customers — those who bought only once — added just $0.12 million across the entire base.

The shop was spending on stock, staff, and floor space to serve a large volume of customers who contributed almost nothing to the bottom line. The real business was being carried by a group so small it was easy to overlook.

This pattern is not unique to bicycle retail. It shows up in F&B, fashion, and e-commerce again and again.

The question worth asking your own business: what percentage of your customers have come back more than twice? If most of them haven't, you are processing orders — not building a customer base.

Those two things look similar from the outside. They produce very different businesses over time.
