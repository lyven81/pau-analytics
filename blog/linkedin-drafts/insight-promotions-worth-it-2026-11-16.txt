Your biggest promotion may be your least profitable one.

A homeware retailer in KL ran three promotions over nine months. Each time, sales rose by around 31%. Each time, net profit fell by about 8%.

Then she ran a Mother's Day promotion — and the numbers went the other way.

She didn't do a storewide discount. She built three gift bundles: a bedding set, a kitchen storage kit, a candle arrangement. Limited time, 15% off. No blanket sale.

Revenue rose 24% that weekend — slightly lower than her usual promotions.
Net profit rose 6%.

One number explains why.

Customers from the storewide 20–30% discount events came back within 90 days at a rate of 11%.
Customers from the gift bundle promotion came back at 29%.

The big discounts brought price buyers. They came for the deal and left when the deal ended. The bundle promotion brought buyers with a specific need — people who were shopping for a purpose. Those people returned.

The promotion that looked smaller on the revenue line was the one actually building the customer base.

If you've run three or more promotions in the last year, check the 90-day return rate for each one. The answer about which one was actually worth running is already in your data.
