Is Your Pricing Right? What Data From a Malaysian Café Chain Reveals

A café chain owner in Kuala Lumpur ran three outlets for four years. Her prices were set to match the competition — she checked the market every six months and adjusted when needed. Business was steady, but profit margins were thinner than expected given her volumes. She brought in Pau Analytics to find out whether her pricing was actually working in her favour.

The assumption most business owners carry is that if customers are paying the price, the price must be right. But paying a price and the price being optimal are two different things. Pau Analytics reviewed 14,800 transactions across her three outlets over 12 months, examining each menu item for price elasticity — how much customer demand actually changed in response to price movement — and for the profit each item contributed at different price levels.

What the Data Showed

The analysis split the menu into two clear groups. The first group — 38% of items — showed very low price sensitivity. Customer order volume barely moved when prices changed. Her RM8.50 white coffee and RM9.00 teh tarik were pulling the same number of orders regardless. A modelled 15% increase on these two items would have had minimal impact on units sold, meaning she was leaving measurable profit on the table every single day without knowing it.

The second group showed clear price resistance. Her RM16.90 dessert platter — the signature item on the menu — had lost 31% of its orders compared to a test period when it was priced at RM14.90. The extra RM2.00 was costing more in lost volume than it gained in margin improvement. The owner had held the higher price because revenue per unit looked better. The data showed the total profit contribution had actually declined.

What Changed

Pau Analytics recommended three specific actions. First, raise the white coffee and teh tarik by RM1.00 each — the data confirmed customers would not reduce their orders. Second, bring the dessert platter back to RM14.90 and recover the lost volume. Third, introduce a weekday afternoon bundle running from 2pm to 5pm at a modest discount, converting the outlet's quietest traffic window into a net positive contributor rather than a cost that ran at near-zero revenue.

The owner implemented all three changes. Within two months, average transaction value across the three outlets increased by RM1.80 per bill — a 9.4% improvement. No new menu items were added. No promotions were run. No additional staff were hired. The dessert platter recovered to its previous order volume. The afternoon bundle created a revenue window that had previously been almost entirely empty.

The Result

The owner now reviews pricing quarterly using a dashboard that tracks order volume, revenue per item, and margin contribution by time window and outlet. An AI assistant supports her in identifying which items are showing early signs of price resistance and which may have room to move without affecting demand. Pricing decisions at the chain are now driven by what the transaction data shows customers have already decided — not by what competitors are charging or what feels right.
